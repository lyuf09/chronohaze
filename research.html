Optimization Research · Duality / Lower Bounds

Spring 2026 | A New Research Direction (Cornell ORIE)

Problem: Understand which dual / lower-bound framework gives nontrivial information for a graph-relative-difference distance-fitting objective (B-encoded), with current focus on constrained/composite first-order structure and duality.

Method: Compare DC/Fenchel-based dualization against plain Lagrangian duals, and analyze where relaxation becomes too loose (unconstrainedness / translation invariance / non-identifiability).

Current status: Right now we’re trying to understand which dual framework gives nontrivial information for this distance-fitting objective. The DC/Fenchel route gives a clean conjugate-based dual with explicit domain conditions, while the most natural plain Lagrangian duals turn out to be too loose and collapse to the trivial bound 0 due to unconstrainedness and translation invariance. The next step is to tighten the relaxation (e.g., via gauge-fixing and/or an augmented Lagrangian / ADMM viewpoint) to obtain an informative bound or an algorithmically useful dual.

Contribution: Dual-framework comparison, structural diagnosis of degeneracy, and research-note documentation of the evolving problem formulation.

What I’m Currently Thinking About

Questions and themes I’m actively using to steer reading, proof work, and modeling choices.

  • Which dual formulations remain informative under non-identifiability / translation invariance, and which collapse to trivial lower bounds.
  • How to design reusable proof structure in Isabelle/HOL so formalization artifacts scale beyond one theorem statement.
  • When a “natural” relaxation is too loose: what structural fix (gauge-fixing, constraints, augmentation) restores useful information.
  • How signal-like inputs (audio, temporal traces) can be mapped into discrete, computable structures without losing interpretability.
  • How optimization / formal methods / modeling can share one language of explicit assumptions and reproducible abstractions.
