Metalcore Piano Lab｜从音频到谱面：一个离散化的实验（WIP）

HazezZ · 2月7日 · 约 3 分钟

我一直很想试试能不能把一段 metalcore / prog-metal 音频直接变成可以玩的音游谱面。音频里的节奏是连续时间上的信号，但是谱面最后得是一串确定时间和轨道的 notes。所以我想看看从音频分析到谱面生成到底需要哪些步骤。

Metalcore Piano Lab 是我做的一个浏览器端原型。它可以读入本地音频或 MIDI，然后尝试生成钢琴式的多轨谱面。目前还在开发，尤其是复杂节奏的识别和谱面的可玩性都还需要调整。

现在能做什么

目前的流程是上传 mp3 / wav / m4a 或 MIDI，点击 Analyze，再用键盘玩生成的谱面。原型支持 4、6、8 keys 和不同难度。

我主要想检查从连续音频到离散谱面的这条流程能不能实际跑通，还有哪一步会把节奏结构弄错。

音频怎么变成谱面

目前大概分成下面几步：

  • 起音检测：先找出鼓点、拨弦和其他比较明显的起音位置。这些事件决定谱面应该在什么地方放 notes。
  • 速度估计和节拍跟踪：根据检测到的事件建立节拍网格，再把 note timing 对齐到网格上。否则即使起音大致正确也可能出现明显的时间漂移。
  • 拍号估计：把事件分成合理的节拍组。Prog-metal 里经常有不规则分组，所以不能一直默认相同的重音模式。
  • 段落识别和模板对齐：我很在意重复 riff 的谱面是不是一致。同一个段落重新出现的时候如果生成了完全不一样的按键模式，实际玩起来会很奇怪，所以需要尽量识别重复段落并复用对齐结果。
  • 可玩性检查：数学上能放下的 notes 不一定适合人打。还得考虑密度、手位、重复按键和跨度。

为什么拿 metalcore 测试

Metalcore 和 prog-metal 里强切分、密集鼓点和不规则拍号都很常见。这些输入会让 beat tracking 和 section alignment 更容易出问题，所以我觉得拿它们测试比只用节奏很规整的音乐更能看出算法的限制。

现在还有什么问题

目前是 browser-native 的轻量实现，没有做完整的 stem separation。所以如果混音很密或者好几种乐器同时在响，起音检测可能会混在一起。

复杂 polymeter、突然变速和强切分也会影响节拍和拍号估计。结果就是部分 notes 可能对不上预期的拍点，或者同一个 riff 的两次谱面不够一致。

接下来想改什么

  • 改善变速和强切分情况下的 tempo / beat tracking。
  • 加强 sectioning 和 template alignment，减少重复段落的差异。
  • 重新整理 difficulty scaling，把密度、跨度和手位负担对应到不同难度。
  • 增加对不合理连打、过大跳跃和连续手位切换的限制。
  • 把 offset 和 latency 校准做得更细，避免谱面分析正确但判定时间不对。

我现在更关心生成出来的谱面实际好不好玩，而不只是能不能从音频里检测到足够多的事件。

Metalcore Piano Lab | From Audio to Chart: A Discretization Experiment (WIP)

HazezZ · 7 February 2026 · ~3 min read

I have wanted to see whether I could turn a metalcore or prog-metal track directly into a playable rhythm-game chart. The music is recorded as a continuous audio signal, but the chart needs notes at specific times and in specific lanes. I wanted to work out what needs to happen between those two things.

Metalcore Piano Lab is a browser-based prototype I've been making. It takes local audio or MIDI files and tries to generate multi-lane, piano-style charts. It's still in development. Recognising complicated rhythms and making the charts comfortable to play both need more work.

What it can do so far

You can upload an MP3, WAV, M4A or MIDI file, click Analyze, and play the generated chart on your keyboard. It has 4-, 6- and 8-key layouts and several difficulty levels.

I mainly want to check whether the whole process from audio to chart actually works, and where it starts getting the rhythm wrong.

How the audio becomes a chart

At the moment, there are roughly five steps:

  • Onset detection: Find drum hits, picked notes and other noticeable attacks. These give me possible positions for notes in the chart.
  • Tempo estimation and beat tracking: Build a beat grid from the detected events, then align the notes to it. Otherwise the timing can drift even when the detected onsets are reasonably accurate.
  • Time-signature estimation: Group the events into beats and bars that make sense. Prog-metal often uses irregular groupings, so I can't assume the same accent pattern all the way through.
  • Section detection and template alignment: I care quite a lot about whether repeated riffs produce similar charts. Playing the same section twice with completely different key patterns would feel strange, so the program needs to recognise repeated passages and align them.
  • Playability checks: A chart can be generated correctly in terms of timing but still be awkward to play. I also have to consider note density, hand positions, repeated key presses and large jumps.

Why use metalcore for testing?

Metalcore and prog-metal often have heavy syncopation, dense drums and unusual time signatures. These are exactly the things that make beat tracking and section alignment difficult. I think testing with this music tells me more about the program's limitations than only trying tracks with very regular rhythms.

What still needs work

This is a lightweight implementation that runs in the browser, without full stem separation. When a mix is dense or several instruments play at once, the onset detector can confuse their attacks.

Complicated polymeters, sudden tempo changes and strong syncopation can also affect the beat and time-signature estimates. Some notes end up off the intended beat, and repeated riffs sometimes produce inconsistent patterns.

What I want to change next

  • Improve tempo and beat tracking when the tempo changes or the rhythm is heavily syncopated.
  • Improve section detection and template alignment so repeated passages produce more consistent charts.
  • Adjust difficulty scaling using note density, hand movement and the distance between keys.
  • Reduce unnecessary repeated presses, large jumps and awkward changes in hand position.
  • Calibrate timing offset and latency more carefully so the hit timing is right even when the analysis is accurate.

Right now I care more about whether the chart is actually fun to play than how many events the program manages to detect.
