Spring 2026｜新科研记录的开始

Feier Lyu · 1月29日 · 约 2 分钟

2026 年春季学期我开始在康奈尔大学 ORIE 系做本科科研，导师是 Shoham Sabach 教授。

在这之前我已经做过一些 Isabelle 形式化相关的工作，也有过其他数学方向的研究训练。但是这次我想更系统地学一阶优化方法，然后尝试自己研究一个具体的问题。

上学期我主要是在 Sabach 教授的指导下读文献和学习相关理论，也上了他教的优化课程。这学期我还选了他的 Optimization for AI，内容挺有意思的 :)

我本科主要学纯数学，所以刚开始接触这些优化论文的时候还需要熟悉它们的算法定义、假设和收敛证明。Sabach 教授也有纯数学背景，转向优化研究之后一直做相关理论。这点让我觉得交流起来很顺畅，而且他愿意让我先读、先尝试，然后一起讨论哪些问题值得继续做。

这学期我们计划研究约束优化和复合优化的一阶方法（first-order methods for constrained and composite optimization）。目前最重要的是找到一个范围合适、可以认真推导的具体问题，而不是急着把研究方向写得很大。

对我来说这也是一次从阅读已有证明到尝试做新问题的过渡。我会把后续比较值得记录的推导和想法整理下来。

Spring 2026 | A New Research Direction

Feier Lyu · 29 January 2026 · ~2 min read

In spring 2026 I started undergraduate research in Cornell's School of Operations Research and Information Engineering (ORIE), supervised by Professor Shoham Sabach.

Before this, I had worked on some Isabelle formalisation projects and had research experience in other areas of maths. This time I wanted to study first-order optimisation methods more systematically and try investigating a specific problem myself.

Last semester I mainly read papers and learned the relevant theory with Professor Sabach. I also took his optimisation course. This semester I'm taking his Optimization for AI course as well, which I've been enjoying :)

Most of my undergraduate courses are in pure maths, so when I first started reading optimisation papers I needed some time to get used to how they define algorithms, state their assumptions and prove convergence. Professor Sabach also has a pure-maths background and later moved into optimisation research. That has made it easy for us to talk through the maths. He lets me read and try things on my own before we discuss which questions are worth pursuing.

Our plan this semester is to study first-order methods for constrained and composite optimisation. The important thing for now is to find a specific problem that's manageable but still has enough maths to work through properly. I don't want to rush into something too broad.

For me, this is also a chance to move from reading other people's proofs to trying a new research problem. I'll write down the calculations and ideas that seem worth keeping as I go.
