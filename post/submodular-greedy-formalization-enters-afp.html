子模贪心算法形式化正式进入 AFP

Feier Lyu · Originally posted 2026年6月30日 · 约 6 分钟

Isabelle/HOL, submodular maximization, greedy algorithms, approximation guarantees, AFP

Published: Archive of Formal Proofs · 2026年5月26日。

AFP entry DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21054718 GitHub repository

2026 年 5 月 26 日，我的 Isabelle/HOL 项目 Greedy Algorithms for Cardinality-Constrained Submodular Maximization 发表于 Archive of Formal Proofs（AFP）。这篇文章简单记录一下项目的内容，以及我在形式化过程中遇到的几个具体问题。

研究对象是有限 ground set 上带基数约束的单调非负子模最大化。在相应的假设下，classical greedy 经过 k 步可以得到 1 − (1 − 1/k)k 的有限步近似保证，因此也满足熟悉的 1 − 1/e 近似比。AFP 条目还包含一个经过验证的 stateful lazy greedy 版本：它维护边际增益的缓存上界，在不必重新计算所有候选元素的情况下，仍然能选出符合 greedy 条件的元素。

发表范围：AFP 收录的是 classical greedy 的形式化构造与近似证明，以及 stateful lazy greedy 的正确性和近似保证。仓库里后来加入的 StochasticGreedy，包括采样模型、近似证明和 oracle 调用次数的分析，是发表后的扩展，不属于这个 AFP 条目。

如果只写在纸上，classical greedy 的证明并不长：每一步选取边际增益最大的元素，用子模性得到单步增益的下界，再通过递推不等式推出近似比。但在 Isabelle 里，我还需要处理一些很具体的条件：ground set 是不是有限的，候选元素是否存在，当前集合是否满足基数约束，以及实际选出的元素是否符合贪心步骤的定义。这些在手写证明里很容易被略过。

我把形式化分成了三个部分：可执行的 greedy 构造、描述合法贪心步骤的抽象条件，以及后面的近似分析。先证明具体的选择过程满足单步条件，再复用相应的近似证明。这样如果以后换一种实现，只需要重新核对它的选择步骤，不必把整个近似分析再证明一遍。

Lazy greedy 的部分需要额外处理算法状态。普通 greedy 每次直接比较真实的边际增益；lazy greedy 则保存一些上界，只在必要时更新。形式化时，我需要证明这些缓存值始终是有效上界，并且最终接受的元素确实满足 greedy 的选择条件。证明这两点之后，lazy greedy 才能沿用 classical greedy 的近似保证。

我之前就学过这个近似定理，但真正写进 Isabelle 之后，花时间最多的并不是最后那一步推导，而是前面的定义和引理。哪些地方用了单调性，哪些地方用了子模性，哪些条件应该放在通用的接口里，哪些只属于具体算法，都需要分清楚。我觉得这也是这次项目对我最有用的部分。

很感谢 Wenda Li 老师在项目中的指导，也感谢 Tobias Nipkow 老师在最后整理和 AFP 发表阶段提供的帮助。看到自己的形式化项目正式出现在 AFP 上，我很开心。

发表时，我还想继续形式化其他一阶优化和组合优化中的经典证明，并尽量把已有的定义和引理整理得方便复用。后来仓库中的 StochasticGreedy 工作，也是在这个基础上继续扩展的。

做完这次项目，我现在看算法证明会更留意每一步具体依赖了什么，而不只是最后得到了哪个定理。

Formalizing Submodular Greedy Is Now in the AFP

Feier Lyu · Originally posted June 30, 2026 · 7 min read

Isabelle/HOL, submodular maximization, greedy algorithms, approximation guarantees, AFP

Published: Archive of Formal Proofs · May 26, 2026.

AFP entry DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21054718 GitHub repository

On 26 May 2026, my Isabelle/HOL project Greedy Algorithms for Cardinality-Constrained Submodular Maximization was published in the Archive of Formal Proofs (AFP). I wanted to record what I formalised and a few details that needed more care than they would in a handwritten proof.

The problem is monotone, non-negative submodular maximisation over a finite ground set, subject to a cardinality constraint. Under the theorem's assumptions, classical greedy has the finite-step approximation factor 1 − (1 − 1/k)k after k steps, which gives the familiar 1 − 1/e guarantee. The AFP entry also includes a verified stateful lazy-greedy variant. It maintains cached upper bounds on marginal gains so that it can make a valid greedy choice without evaluating every candidate again at every step.

Publication scope: The AFP entry covers classical greedy, its approximation proof, and the correctness and approximation guarantee of stateful lazy greedy. The StochasticGreedy work added to the repository later—including sampling, approximation results, and oracle-call bounds—is a post-publication extension, not part of this AFP entry.

The usual handwritten greedy proof is fairly short: choose an element with maximum marginal gain, use submodularity to bound the progress at each step, and solve the resulting recurrence. In Isabelle, I also had to account for the finiteness of the ground set, the existence of a candidate, preservation of the cardinality constraint, and whether the selected element actually satisfies the greedy-step specification. It is easy to leave these points implicit on paper.

I separated the development into an executable greedy construction, an abstract specification of a valid greedy step, and the approximation argument. Once a concrete implementation is shown to satisfy the step specification, the approximation proof can be reused. This avoids proving the same recurrence again for every implementation.

Lazy greedy required some additional work with algorithm state. Rather than recomputing all marginal gains at every iteration, it stores upper bounds and updates them when needed. The formal proof has to establish that those bounds remain valid and that the element eventually accepted is a valid greedy choice. With those properties in place, the lazy implementation inherits the classical approximation guarantee.

I knew the approximation theorem before starting this project. What took most of my time in Isabelle was not the final inequality but the definitions and intermediate lemmas: where monotonicity is used, where submodularity is needed, and which assumptions belong to a reusable interface rather than to one implementation. Working through those details was probably the most useful part for me.

I'm grateful to Wenda Li for his guidance throughout the project, and to Tobias Nipkow for his help with the final revisions and AFP publication. It was lovely to see the development published as an AFP entry.

At the time of publication, I wanted to continue formalising classical proofs in first-order and combinatorial optimisation, while making the existing definitions and lemmas easier to reuse. The later StochasticGreedy work in the repository builds on that development.

Since this project, I have paid much more attention to the exact assumptions behind each step of an algorithmic proof, not just its final theorem.
