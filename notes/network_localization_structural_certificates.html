网络定位中的二阶几何：研究概览

Feier Lyu · 最初发布于 2026-07-22 · 更新于 2026-08-23 · 约 4 分钟

研究参与者：Feier Lyu、Prof. Shoham Sabach、Owen Li

Network Localization · Nonconvex Optimization · Negative Curvature · Structured Screening

我们现在在研究网络定位问题里可微驻点附近的二阶性质，主要想知道不同边的几何关系会怎样影响 Hessian。

这项合作研究仍在进行，技术结果尚未公开。

最近我一直在研究下面这类网络定位问题：

F(x) = ∑(i,j)∈E (‖xi−xj‖−δij)2.

我们知道图上部分边的目标距离 δij，需要找到节点的位置 xi，让实际距离尽量接近目标距离。这是一个非凸优化问题，所以算法找到 stationary point 并不代表已经找到了局部极小点。

之前已经有工作研究过非光滑 critical points，还有某些情况下从坏点逃逸的构造。我现在主要关注可微的 stationary points：除了直接看 Hessian 的特征值，我们能不能从图结构和节点的几何关系判断有没有负曲率方向？

1. 先看单条边

最开始我想知道一条边的实际长度和目标距离之间的差值能不能决定驻点的二阶性质。这个差值确实会影响这条边在 Hessian 里的贡献，但是只看一条边还不能判断整个 Hessian 有没有负方向。

因为最后需要把所有边的二阶贡献加起来。一条边单独有负的曲率贡献不代表整个 Hessian 就一定有负特征值。

2. 还得把其他边算进去

移动一个节点会同时改变所有连到这个节点的测量边。所以某条边在一个扰动方向上的负贡献可能被其他边的正贡献抵消。我们需要看的是整个网络沿同一个方向的二阶变化，不能把每条边分开判断。

这也是为什么我不想只靠数值计算 Hessian 的最小特征值。这样确实可以检查一个具体算例有没有负曲率，但是我更想知道到底是哪些图结构和几何条件造成了这个结果。

3. 现在做到哪里

我们还在研究可微驻点处的二阶性质与图结构的联系，以及哪些条件能帮助我们判断负曲率。

这个项目是我和 Prof. Shoham Sabach、Owen Li 一起做的。

Second-Order Geometry in Network Localization: A Research Overview

Feier Lyu · Originally posted 2026-07-22 · Updated 2026-08-23 · 4 min read

Research participants: Feier Lyu, Prof. Shoham Sabach, and Owen Li

Network Localization · Nonconvex Optimization · Negative Curvature · Structured Screening

We are looking at the second-order behaviour of a network-localisation objective near differentiable stationary points. More specifically, we want to understand how the geometry of different edges affects the Hessian.

This collaborative project is ongoing; technical results are not yet public.

Recently I've been looking at network-localisation problems of the form:

F(x) = ∑(i,j)∈E (‖xi−xj‖−δij)2.

We know the target distances δij for some edges of a graph. The task is to find positions xi so that the actual distances are as close as possible to those targets. This is a nonconvex problem, so finding a stationary point does not mean we have found a local minimum.

Previous work has looked at nonsmooth critical points, including escape constructions in certain cases. I'm mainly interested in differentiable stationary points. Apart from directly checking the Hessian eigenvalues, can we use the graph structure and node geometry to tell whether a negative-curvature direction exists?

1. Starting with one edge

I first wanted to know whether the difference between an edge's actual length and its target distance could determine the second-order behaviour at a stationary point. It does affect that edge's contribution to the Hessian. But one edge alone is not enough to determine whether the full Hessian has a negative direction.

We need to add the second-order contributions of all the edges. A negative contribution from one edge does not necessarily give the full Hessian a negative eigenvalue.

2. The other edges matter too

Moving one node also changes every measured edge attached to it. So a negative contribution from one edge along a particular direction may be cancelled by positive contributions from others. We have to consider the second-order change of the whole network along the same direction, not each edge separately.

This is why I don't want to rely only on numerical checks of the smallest Hessian eigenvalue. Those checks can tell us whether a particular example has negative curvature, but I also want to know which graph structures or geometric conditions explain the result.

3. Current work

We're investigating how second-order behaviour at differentiable stationary points depends on the graph, and which conditions help us detect negative curvature.

I'm working on this project with Prof. Shoham Sabach and Owen Li.
